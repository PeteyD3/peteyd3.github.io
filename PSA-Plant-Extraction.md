# Technical Feasibility & Process Safety Assessment: Solvent-Based Extraction and Kupchan Fractionation of Peppermint Plant Tissue
**Document ID:** PSA-BIO-109  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** August 31, 2026  
**Prepared By:** Peter Duncan, Technical Writer  

### 1.0 Executive Summary & Project Feasibility
This report evaluates the operational feasibility and safety criteria for isolating bioactive organic compounds from *Mentha x piperita* (peppermint) leaves. The process utilizes raw methanol extraction, qualitative reagent screening, and liquid-liquid Kupchan fractionation to separate compounds into safe fractions based on fluid polarity. Based on a pilot run yielding **10.79% crude mass recovery** (0.1099 grams of purified residue from 1.0185 grams of wet plant tissue), this process is classified as a highly feasible method for early-stage natural product identification.

### 2.0 Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) & Workplace Hazards
> ⚠️ **CRITICAL FLAMMABILITY HAZARD:** Methanol and ethyl acetate are highly volatile, Class 1 flammable solvents with low flash points. All procedures must occur inside an explosion-proof chemical fume hood with zero active ignition sources.

#### 2.1 Reagent Toxicity Protocols
*   **Methanol:** Toxic if swallowed or inhaled. Operators must wear full nitrile hand protections to eliminate skin absorption routes.
*   **Tollens' Reagent:** Contains silver nitrate and ammonia. Waste streams must neutralize safely to prevent the spontaneous formation of explosive silver fulminate precipitates.
*   **Lucas Reagent:** Contains concentrated hydrochloric acid and zinc chloride. Highly corrosive to human tissue and metal structural framing.

### 3.0 Standardized Qualitative Testing Protocol (Process Controls)
To monitor the extraction stream and identify active functional components, operators must execute four targeted chemical verification tests using exactly 0.5 mL of reagent mixed with 1 drop of liquid analyte:

1.  **Aldehyde Control (Tollens' Test):** Positive validation is marked by a deep grey or silver mirror precipitate, signaling target sugar or aldehyde oxidation.
2.  **Carbonyl Control (2,4-DNP Test):** Positive validation yields an immediate bright orange color shift, confirming the presence of aldehydes or ketone structures.
3.  **Alcohol Matrix Control (Lucas Test):** Positive validation is characterized by an immediate dark, oily yellow layer separation, confirming secondary or tertiary hydroxyl links.
4.  **Phenolic Matrix Control (Ferric Chloride Test):** Negative runs show a clear yellow/green fluid profile, confirming the complete absence of acidic phenol rings in the matrix stream.

### 4.0 Kupchan Liquid-Liquid Partitioning Workflow
To systematically separate the multi-component plant extract based on mass-transfer polarity boundaries, run the following automated extraction sequences:

1.  **Prepare Feed Phase:** Reconstitute the crude plant mass into a 5.0 mL aqueous solution containing a strict 9:1 methanol-to-distilled-water ratio.
2.  **First Extraction (Nonpolar Cut):** Introduce 5.0 mL of industrial hexane into the vessel. Agitate vigorously 5 to 6 times using a pipette to create a high surface-area emulsion. Allow phases to separate cleanly, then isolate the top nonpolar hexane layer to concentrate low-polarity lipids.
3.  **Second Extraction (Polar Cut):** Dilute the remaining bottom aqueous phase with 4.0 mL of distilled water to shift the solvent dielectric constant. Add 5.0 mL of ethyl acetate and mix uniformly. Drain the resulting top organic phase to isolate moderately polar target compounds.

### 5.0 Quality Verification Parameters
The extraction process is considered verified and in control if thin-layer chromatography (TLC) under short-wave UV light (254 nm) yields exactly six distinct component bands from the crude extract across a 1:1 Hexane:Ethyl Acetate mobile phase. It must map perfectly to the baseline target Retention Factor (\(Rf\)) range of **0.40 to 0.92**.
